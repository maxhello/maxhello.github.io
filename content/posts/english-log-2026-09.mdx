## What I notice

The score went up five points, but I do not feel five points better.

The Duolingo score is a progress meter, not a test: one point for every three units. And these units were easy — plural nouns, negatives, present continuous. The placement test in July put me at about 20; I chose "too hard" and dropped back to 10, so September was mostly review. Reading is fast now, but writing from Chinese is still slow and listening is still weak.

Also: 36 hours, almost all of it tapping words. Duolingo keeps me in contact with English every day, but it does not make me speak. That is why the score moves and the feeling does not.

## How long is this going to take?

My pace is 14 units a month. If it stays there:

| Stage | Units left | Done by |
|---|---|---|
| A1, second section | 30 | early Dec 2026 |
| A2 | 60 | Apr 2027 |
| B1 | 515 | 2030 |
| B2 | 360 | 2032 |

Duolingo calls A2 "simple everyday conversation" and B1 "independent in daily life". So daily English is 6–7 months away, not the 4 I had in my head, and B1 at this pace is not realistic. There is not much left to skip, either: the easy units were me re-learning what I had stepped back from, and from here the material is new. The change that matters is outside the app — real speaking practice.

Learning English is hard because there is no shortcut, just time. But the hardest part, showing up every day, is already working.

## October goals

- Hold the pace in the second A1 section, even as the units stop being review.
- Write two or three sentences of my own from each unit's key sentences, every day.
- Start speaking practice, even 10 minutes a day.
- Keep the streak.
