## What I notice

This was the lightest month so far. Lessons and time are both about a quarter below September, and 52 minutes a day is the lowest since I started. But the streak did not break, and I am fine with that trade: September was 72 minutes a day of mostly review. October was new material at a slower pace.

The units did get harder, as I expected last month. The first A1 section was things I had already seen in the placement test. The second section is not. 11 units instead of 14 is the cost of that.

The score moved three points, one for every three or four units, exactly the way the meter is built. I still do not read the score as a level. It is a count of finished units with a CEFR label on it.

## Super, the Indian way

Duolingo prices its subscription by country, and India is one of the cheapest. In October I spent some time trying to buy the family plan there, which would be the lowest price per person. It did not work out in the end. I did get an individual Super subscription on the Indian store, so the ads are gone and the hearts are unlimited. That is a small thing, but a lesson that stops because you ran out of hearts is a lesson you did not do.

## I cannot really speak

This is the main thing I learned in October, and it is not a nice one: my speaking is very weak.

After 97 days and more than 80,000 XP, I can read the sentences in my units and build them from word tiles. But when I try to say the same sentence out loud, without the tiles, it comes out slowly, with the wrong sounds, or not at all. Words I "know" in Duolingo are words I can recognize, not words I can say.

It is not really Duolingo's fault. Almost every exercise is tapping, matching or typing, about three and a half minutes per lesson, and I have done more than 1,500 of them. I trained my eyes and my thumbs. Nobody trained my mouth. Duolingo is good at what it does for me: it brings me back every day, and it gives me words and grammar in small steps. I will keep it. But Duolingo alone is not enough.

## Looking for a second app

So at the end of the month I started looking for something to put next to Duolingo, only for speaking.

The first one I tried was [Speak](https://www.speak.com/). It is built around talking out loud: it listens, corrects, and makes you repeat. That is exactly what I am missing. But the A1 lessons are full of names of people and places, and those are the words I can read the least. I kept getting stuck on a name instead of practicing the sentence, and it felt awkward. A good app, I think, but not a good fit for where I am.

What I am looking for:

- Speaking first, not tapping.
- Words I already know, so the hard part is the mouth, not the vocabulary.
- Explanations in Chinese, or no reading at all.
- Short enough to do every day next to Duolingo.

I am comparing a few options now. If you learned to speak English, or any language, as an adult beginner and something worked for you — an app, a method, a habit — please tell me in the comments below. I would really like to hear it.
