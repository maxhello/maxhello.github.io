I published [lp-lab](https://github.com/maxhello/lp-lab) — a shift scheduling
engine that takes your staff, your rules and your coverage needs, and returns a
fair, legal schedule in milliseconds. Try the
[live demo](https://lp-lab.onrender.com/) (free tier: first hit after idle
takes ~30–60s to wake up).

## The problem

Employee scheduling looks like a spreadsheet problem until you actually try to
fill one in. A one-week plan for a small team has to satisfy all of the
following at once:

- **Coverage**: every day × shift slot needs between min and max headcount,
  sometimes with required skills ("every late shift needs at least one
  CPR-certified employee").
- **Labor rules**: max consecutive working days, minimum rest between shifts
  (including cross-midnight ones), weekly hour caps, at most one shift per day.
- **Fairness**: nobody should silently collect all the weekend or night shifts.
- **Preferences**: preferred shifts where possible, time-off requests respected.

Check any two of those by hand and it is fine. Satisfy all of them at once,
week after week, across a whole team — that is a combinatorial search problem,
and humans solve it by repeatedly breaking the rule nobody is watching.

This is exactly what constraint programming eats for breakfast.

## The model

lp-lab is a thin, careful layer on top of
[Google OR-Tools](https://developers.google.com/optimization) CP-SAT. Every
`(employee, day, shift)` triple becomes one boolean variable, and each rule is
a small constraint over those booleans:

| Rule | Encoding |
| --- | --- |
| Coverage | `min ≤ Σ assigns ≤ max` per slot |
| Required skill | `Σ qualified-employee assigns ≥ 1` |
| One shift/day | `Σ shift assigns ≤ 1` per employee-day |
| Consecutive days | sliding-window sum ≤ limit |
| Min rest | forbidden adjacent-day shift pairs (cross-midnight aware) |
| Weekly cap | `Σ assigns × duration ≤ cap` per 7-day window |
| Hard time-off | variable fixed to 0 |

The example scenario in the README — 3 employees, 3 shift types, a week of
coverage with skills and time-off — solves in about **24 ms**, and CP-SAT
tells you whether the answer is `OPTIMAL`, merely `FEASIBLE`, or provably
`INFEASIBLE`. That last one matters: instead of "the solver didn't find
anything", you get a machine-checkable statement that *no* valid schedule
exists under these rules, and a hint about why.

## Fairness is the interesting part

Balancing total hours is the naive version of fairness. The subtle failure
mode is **cross-dimensional**: suppose there are 4 weekends of shifts to cover
and 5 employees — weekend rest cannot be split evenly, so someone loses their
weekend no matter what. Hours-only fairness is blind to that.

lp-lab converts weekend shifts, night shifts and hours into a single
**hardship score** (weekend shift = 480, night shift = 240, one hour = 60) and
minimises the spread of that score. When weekend rest cannot be divided
evenly, the person who loses their weekend gets compensated with fewer nights
or a lighter load — automatically, because the objective function sees all
three dimensions as one currency.

Preferences ride along in the same objective: soft time-off violations are
penalised, preferred-shift matches are rewarded, all with configurable
weights.

## Failing fast, and not trusting the solver

Two engineering choices worth calling out:

**`/api/validate`** runs cheap pre-checks before you ever invoke the solver —
things like "min_staff exceeds the number of skill-qualified employees". Most
impossible setups are configuration mistakes, not deep infeasibility, and they
should fail in microseconds with a human-readable message, not after a solve
attempt.

**Tests recompute everything.** For every hard constraint, the test suite
independently recomputes the property (rest hours, consecutive days, coverage,
caps…) directly from the returned assignments. The solver's own claims about
its solutions are never trusted. Solvers are extremely good at what they do,
but the moment you treat "it returned a schedule" as "it returned a *valid*
schedule", you have a production incident waiting for its first shift swap.

## Product shape

- **Web UI + REST API**, no database — input auto-saves to localStorage,
  results export to CSV in one click.
- The UI is a plain form over the same JSON the API accepts; nothing hidden.
